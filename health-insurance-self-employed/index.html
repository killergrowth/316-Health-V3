Health Insurance for the Self-Employed in Kansas

One of the most common things we hear from self-employed clients: “I’ve been putting this off because I didn’t know where to start.” Between income variability, subsidy calculations, and the general sense that it’s more complicated for freelancers and contractors, a lot of people just avoid it until something forces the issue.

We work with a lot of self-employed Kansans — 1099 workers, consultants, sole proprietors, freelancers — and the situation isn’t as complicated as it feels. The right coverage and the right strategy depends on your income, your family situation, and how your business is structured. We help you sort through it in plain language and find something that actually works.

Your main options as a self-employed person

Self-employed individuals have access to the same health insurance markets as everyone else, but the right strategy often looks different based on your income, business structure, and family situation. The primary options are:

ACA Marketplace Plans

Available to anyone, with potential premium tax credits based on income. Income variability as a self-employed person can make subsidy estimation tricky -- we help you navigate this.

Off-Marketplace Plans

Can offer more flexibility if your income is too high to qualify for subsidies.

Health Sharing Plans

Not insurance, but worth understanding as an alternative. We explain the difference and the risks.

Small Group Coverage

If you have at least one employee, you may qualify for a small group plan, which can offer different benefit structures.

The self-employed health insurance deduction

One tax advantage unique to self-employed individuals: you can typically deduct 100% of health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your spouse, and your dependents from your gross income, regardless of whether you itemize. This deduction can significantly reduce the real cost of your coverage. We factor this in when helping you evaluate total cost of ownership for different plan options. (Consult your tax professional for specifics, we are health insurance brokers, not CPAs.)

Managing coverage with variable income

Income variability is one of the real challenges for self-employed people on an ACA plan. Your subsidy is based on what you project your income will be. If the actual number comes in higher, you may owe back part of your credit at tax time. If it comes in lower, you may get more. We help you think through income scenarios, understand the risk on both sides, and decide how conservative to be with your estimate. It’s a conversation worth having before you enroll, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Can I deduct health insurance premiums if I’m self-employed?
    Generally yes, self-employed individuals can typically deduct 100% of health insurance premiums paid for themselves, their spouse, and dependents from gross income. This is above-the-line, so you don’t need to itemize. Consult your tax professional to confirm your specific situation qualifies.
  • My income varies. How do I estimate subsidies?
    Income estimation for ACA subsidies is based on your projected annual income. If your actual income ends up higher, you may owe back part of your advance premium tax credit when you file taxes. We walk you through different income scenarios and help you decide how conservative to be with your estimate.
  • Can I get group coverage if I have a solo business?
    In most cases, you need at least one W-2 employee (other than yourself as owner) to qualify for a small group plan in Kansas. As a solo self-employed person, you would typically purchase individual coverage rather than a group plan.
  • What if I just went from a W-2 job to self-employment?
    Losing job-based coverage is a qualifying life event that triggers a Special Enrollment Period, typically 60 days from your last day of employer coverage. During this window, you can enroll in an ACA Marketplace plan or other individual coverage without waiting for Open Enrollment. We recommend acting quickly to avoid a coverage gap.
