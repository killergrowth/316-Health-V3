ACA & Marketplace Health Insurance Plans in Kansas

A lot of people come to us after spending hours on the Marketplace website, picking a plan that seemed fine, and then discovering it wasn’t when they actually needed to use it. The wrong deductible. The wrong network. A subsidy estimate that came back to bite them at tax time.

The ACA Marketplace isn’t complicated because you’re not smart enough to figure it out. It’s complicated because it was designed by the federal government. The good news: you don’t have to navigate it alone. At 316, we help Kansans sort through Marketplace plans every day. We figure out what you qualify for, which plan actually makes sense for your situation, and we handle enrollment. No extra cost to you. The carrier pays our fee.

What is ACA / Marketplace health insurance?

The Affordable Care Act created a regulated marketplace where individuals and families can shop for health insurance. Every plan must cover essential health benefits and can’t deny coverage or charge you more because of a pre-existing condition.

Plans are organized by metal tier: Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum. The metal tier isn’t about quality — it describes how costs are split between your monthly premium and what you pay at the doctor’s office. Bronze means lower monthly cost, higher out-of-pocket when you use it. Platinum is the reverse. Silver sits in the middle and is the only tier eligible for cost-sharing reductions if your income qualifies. That distinction matters more than most people realize.

Premium tax credits and subsidies

Here’s a number worth paying attention to: your subsidy estimate. If your income falls within certain thresholds relative to the federal poverty level, you may qualify for a Premium Tax Credit that significantly reduces your monthly premium. That’s real money.

The problem is that if you estimate your income wrong, you can end up owing that money back when you file your taxes. It’s one of the most common — and most avoidable — mistakes people make when they enroll without help. We calculate your estimated subsidy as part of our consultation and flag the situations where income variability creates risk.

Open enrollment and special enrollment periods

Timing matters. Open Enrollment typically runs November 1 through January 15. Miss that window and you generally can’t enroll until the following year — unless a qualifying life event opens a Special Enrollment Period. Losing job-based coverage, getting married, having a baby, moving, turning 26 — those events trigger a 60-day window to enroll. We track your timeline and make sure you know when you need to act before the window closes.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is a metal tier and which one should I choose?
    Metal tiers describe the split between your monthly premium and what you pay when you use healthcare. Bronze = lower monthly cost, higher cost at the doctor. Platinum = the reverse. Silver is the only tier eligible for cost-sharing reductions if your income qualifies — which can make it significantly more valuable than its premium alone suggests. The right tier depends on how often you use healthcare, what your income looks like, and what financial exposure you can reasonably absorb. We walk through those numbers with you.
  • Do I qualify for a subsidy on the ACA Marketplace?
    Possibly — and more people qualify than expect to. Subsidies are based on your income relative to the federal poverty level and the size of your household. Even moderate incomes can qualify for meaningful premium assistance. We calculate your estimated subsidy as part of our consultation, and we flag the income scenarios where you need to be careful about over- or under-estimating.
  • What is a Special Enrollment Period?
    A Special Enrollment Period (SEP) lets you enroll in a Marketplace plan outside of Open Enrollment when a qualifying life event occurs — losing job-based coverage, turning 26, getting married, having a baby, moving to a new coverage area. You typically have 60 days from the event. Miss that window and you’re likely waiting until the next Open Enrollment. If you’ve recently had a life change, call us before that clock runs out.
  • Does using a broker cost me more?
    No. The premium you pay is identical whether you enroll through us or on your own. Carriers compensate independent brokers directly — it doesn’t affect your rate. What you get by working with us is someone who actually knows the plans, catches subsidy mistakes before they become tax surprises, and verifies your doctors are in-network before you enroll.
  • Can I keep my current doctors on an ACA plan?
    Maybe — and it’s worth checking before you enroll rather than after. Every plan has its own provider network, and doctors who are in-network on one plan may not be on another. Before we recommend a plan, we verify whether your preferred doctors and hospitals are covered. That’s a standard part of how we work, not an afterthought.
