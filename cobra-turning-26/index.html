COBRA & Turning 26: Health Insurance When Coverage Changes

Coverage transitions are stressful. Whether you just left a job, got laid off, or you’re turning 26 and coming off a parent’s plan — the window to act is 60 days. Most people either don’t know all their options or don’t have time to evaluate them properly under pressure. That’s exactly when people call us.

At 316, we help Kansans figure out what makes the most sense in that window — quickly, clearly, and without pressure. The right answer depends on your specific situation, and we’ve seen enough of them to help you figure out yours.

What is COBRA?

COBRA lets you continue the exact employer health plan you had after a qualifying event — losing a job, having hours reduced, divorce, and others. You keep the same plan, same network, same doctors. The catch: you’re now paying the full premium yourself, including the portion your employer used to cover. That number is often $400–$700+ per month for a single person, and more for a family.

COBRA makes sense in specific situations — usually when you’re mid-treatment, have already met a significant portion of your deductible, or have a specialist relationship you need to preserve. For most people, especially younger and healthier ones, a Marketplace alternative is dramatically cheaper. We run the comparison for your situation before you decide.

Turning 26: getting your own coverage for the first time

Under the ACA, you can stay on a parent’s plan until 26. When that birthday hits, you lose that coverage — and you have a 60-day Special Enrollment Period to get your own. Miss it, and you’re likely waiting until the next Open Enrollment, potentially uninsured for months.

For most 26-year-olds, a Marketplace plan is the right first move — often at a premium that’s lower than expected if your income qualifies for subsidies. What type of plan makes sense depends on your income, how much healthcare you actually use, and which doctors matter to you. We walk through all of it.

COBRA vs. Marketplace: which is right for you?

COBRA
Pros
  • ✓ Same plan, same doctors, same network
  • ✓ No new deductible mid-year (if enrolled within 60 days)
Cons
  • ✗ Full premium, often $400–$700+/month
  • ✗ No subsidy eligibility
  • ✗ Coverage ends at 18 months maximum
ACA Marketplace
Pros
  • ✓ Often $100–$300/month with subsidies
  • ✓ Subsidy eligible based on income
  • ✓ Annual renewal, no hard end date
Cons
  • ✗ New plan and network (may need to change doctors)
  • ✗ New deductible starts from zero

For most people without active treatment or ongoing claims, ACA is cheaper. For people mid-treatment with a deductible already met, COBRA is often worth it short-term. We run the numbers for your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How long do I have to elect COBRA after losing coverage?
    You typically have 60 days from the loss of coverage (or the date you receive your COBRA election notice, whichever is later) to elect continuation coverage. If you miss this window, you lose the option to keep your existing plan through COBRA.
  • Does losing job coverage qualify me for a Special Enrollment Period?
    Yes. Losing employer-sponsored coverage is one of the most common qualifying life events that triggers a 60-day Special Enrollment Period on the ACA Marketplace. You can use this window to compare and enroll in a Marketplace plan even outside of Open Enrollment.
  • Can I switch from COBRA to a Marketplace plan mid-year?
    Yes, voluntarily dropping COBRA is a qualifying event for a Special Enrollment Period on the Marketplace. However, timing matters: you generally have 60 days before your COBRA ends or a specific window when COBRA is voluntarily dropped. We help you plan this transition.
  • I’m turning 26. What do I need to do?
    You lose coverage on your parent’s plan when you turn 26. You have a 60-day Special Enrollment Period from that date to get your own plan. We recommend starting the process at least 30 days before your birthday so coverage is active without a gap. Call us as soon as possible to get started.
  • What happens if I miss both the COBRA window and the Marketplace SEP?
    You would likely be uninsured until the next ACA Open Enrollment period (typically November – January). This is why acting quickly is critical. If you’re approaching a coverage transition, contact us now, do not wait until the last minute.
