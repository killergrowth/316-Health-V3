Individual & Family Health Insurance Plans in Kansas

Most people come to us not knowing what they don’t know. They’ve seen the deductible number on a plan. They’ve looked at the premium. They’re not sure what the rest of it means, and they don’t want to make an expensive mistake by guessing. That’s a reasonable place to be.

At 316, we help individuals and families in Kansas find coverage that actually fits their situation — not just the cheapest number on a comparison tool. We look at your income, your household, your doctors, your medications, and how you tend to use healthcare. Then we compare plans across multiple carriers and explain what you’d actually be getting into. No jargon. No pressure. Just a clear picture of your options.

Who buys individual or family health insurance?

Anyone who isn’t covered through an employer. That includes people who are self-employed, between jobs, early retirees not yet eligible for Medicare, people whose employer doesn’t offer coverage, and people whose employer plan exists but isn’t affordable or doesn’t cover their family. It’s a bigger group than most people think, and the options available are more varied than most people expect.

Understanding plan types: HMO, PPO, EPO, HDHP

Most health insurance plans fall into one of a few structures:

HMO (Health Maintenance Organization)

Requires in-network providers and usually a primary care physician referral to see a specialist. Lower costs, less flexibility.

PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)

See any provider, in or out of network, without a referral. More flexibility, higher potential costs.

EPO (Exclusive Provider Organization)

No referrals required, but must stay in-network. A hybrid between HMO and PPO.

HDHP (High Deductible Health Plan)

Lower premiums, higher deductibles, and eligibility for a Health Savings Account (HSA). Good for healthy individuals who want to build tax-advantaged savings.

The right plan type depends on how often you use healthcare, which providers you want access to, and how you prefer to manage costs. We walk through all of this in our consultation.

How we approach the conversation

We don’t start with products. We start with questions. What are you trying to protect against? Which doctors matter to you? How much healthcare do you actually use in a typical year? Do you have any medications we need to check on a formulary? What can you realistically absorb in a bad year?

From there we identify whether the ACA Marketplace or an off-Marketplace plan makes more sense for your income, compare options across multiple carriers, verify your providers are in-network, and walk you through what each plan actually costs in practice — not just the monthly premium. You leave with a clear recommendation and an understanding of what you’re choosing and why.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What is the difference between a deductible and an out-of-pocket maximum?
    Your deductible is what you pay before insurance starts sharing costs. Your out-of-pocket maximum is the ceiling — the most you’ll pay in a plan year before the insurance covers 100% of covered costs. Both numbers matter, but which one matters more depends on how often you use healthcare. Someone who rarely sees a doctor cares most about the premium and the out-of-pocket max as a backstop. Someone managing a chronic condition cares a lot more about what happens between those two numbers.
  • Should I buy on or off the ACA Marketplace?
    It depends mostly on your income. ACA Marketplace plans can qualify for Premium Tax Credits that significantly reduce your monthly premium — something off-Marketplace plans never do. If your income makes you eligible for subsidies, buying on the Marketplace is almost always the better financial move. We evaluate both options for every client before making a recommendation.
  • Can I add my children or spouse to my plan?
    Yes. Individual plans can cover you, a spouse, and dependent children up to age 26. Your premium is based on who’s on the plan and their ages. We factor your full household into the evaluation so you’re not making coverage decisions in isolation.
  • What if I need to see a doctor before my deductible is met?
    A lot of plans cover certain services before the deductible — preventive care, some primary care visits, generic prescriptions. The specifics vary by plan, and it’s one of the things we look at before recommending a plan to you. Knowing what’s covered on day one vs. after the deductible is cleared matters more than most people realize when they’re comparing options.
  • How long does enrollment take?
    Usually one session, once we have your information. Coverage typically starts the first of the following month. If you’re working within a Special Enrollment Period, the timing rules vary depending on the qualifying event — we walk you through the specifics when that’s the situation.
