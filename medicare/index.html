Medicare Supplement (Medigap) plans

Original Medicare covers a lot, but not everything. Medigap fills the gaps — deductibles, copays, coinsurance — so your costs at time of service are predictable and minimal. One important thing worth knowing: Medigap plans are standardized by the federal government. A Plan G from one carrier covers the same benefits as Plan G from any other. That means the primary difference between carriers is price and service — and we compare both.

With a Medigap supplement, you can see any Medicare-accepting provider in the country. No network restrictions, no referrals needed.

Medicare Part D — prescription drug coverage

If you have Original Medicare plus a Medigap plan, you’ll need a standalone Part D plan for prescription drug coverage. Every Part D plan has its own formulary — a tiered list of covered drugs that determines your cost. A medication covered cheaply on one plan may cost significantly more on another. We check your specific medications against available formularies before recommending a plan.

Missing your Part D enrollment window can result in a permanent late enrollment penalty that follows you for as long as you have coverage. We track your timeline and make sure you know exactly when you need to act.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • What does Medicare Supplement (Medigap) cover?
    Medicare Supplement (Medigap) works alongside Original Medicare to cover the gaps — deductibles, copays, and coinsurance. Plans are standardized by the federal government, so a Plan G from one carrier covers the same benefits as Plan G from any other. With Medigap, you can see any Medicare-accepting provider nationwide with no network restrictions.
  • When can I sign up for Medicare?
    Your Initial Enrollment Period begins three months before the month you turn 65 and ends three months after. Missing this window may result in late enrollment penalties. Special Enrollment Periods exist for people still covered by employer insurance past 65.
  • Is a Medicare broker free to use?
    Yes. Independent Medicare brokers are compensated by the insurance carriers, not by you. You pay the same premium whether you enroll through a broker or directly. The advantage is that we compare options across multiple carriers rather than pushing a single company’s plans.
  • Can I switch Medicare plans after I enroll?
    Yes. The Annual Enrollment Period (AEP) runs October 15 – December 7 each year, and changes take effect January 1. You can also review and switch your Medigap or Part D plan during this window.
  • Do I need a Part D drug plan if I have a Medigap supplement?
    Yes. If you have Original Medicare plus a Medigap supplement, you will need a separate standalone Part D plan for prescription drug coverage. We review drug formularies as part of every Medicare consultation to make sure your medications are covered at the best cost.
